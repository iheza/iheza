Oliver Twist

A Tale of Courage and Kindness
by Charles Dickens · Adapted for Young Readers
📖 Grade 6 · English Literature
Chapter 1 · Oliver's Beginnings

🏚️👶🍼The Workhouse In a dusty old workhouse in a small English town, a baby boy was born. His mother, weak and tired, held him close for just a moment before she passed away. The baby had no family, no home, and not even a name — until the parish officials named him Oliver Twist.

🥣👦😢Hungry Boy Oliver grew up in the workhouse with other orphaned children. They were always hungry. The workhouse master gave them only thin gruel — three meals a day of watery oatmeal. The boys were so hungry that they decided someone must ask for more.

The boys whisper among themselves, pushing Oliver forward.

Oliver Please, sir, I want some more.

The workhouse master stares in shock. His face turns red with anger.

Master More?! You ungrateful boy! How dare you ask for more!

For this terrible crime, Oliver was punished and locked in a dark room. The parish officials decided they must get rid of him. They offered five pounds to anyone who would take Oliver away as an apprentice.

Chapter 2 · A Cruel Master

🧹👦💨The Sweep A chimney sweep named Mr. Gamfield said he would take Oliver. But the kind magistrate saw how frightened Oliver was and refused to let him go. Instead, Oliver was sent to work for an undertaker named Mr. Sowerberry.

Mr. Sowerberry's shop is dark and filled with coffins. Oliver shivers.

Mr. Sowerberry You'll sleep in the shop, boy, among the coffins. And you'll walk in funeral processions.

Oliver worked hard, but the other apprentice, Noah Claypole, was cruel to him. Noah teased Oliver about his mother and made his life miserable. One day, Noah insulted Oliver's mother so terribly that Oliver finally fought back.

Noah Your mother was a bad woman, Oliver Twist! Everybody knows it!

Oliver Don't you speak about my mother! Don't you dare!

Oliver, small but fierce, tackles Noah. Mrs. Sowerberry and Mr. Bumble rush in.

Oliver was punished severely. That night, he packed his few belongings and ran away. He walked for seven days, hungry and tired, until he reached the great city of London.

Chapter 3 · London and a New Friend

🏙️👦🚶Arriving in London London was huge, noisy, and crowded. Oliver had never seen so many people, carriages, and buildings. He was tired, hungry, and had no money. A cheerful boy with a funny hat spotted him sitting on a doorstep.

Jack Dawkins (The Artful Dodger) Hullo, my covey! What's the matter? You look like you've seen hard times!

Oliver I'm very hungry and I have nowhere to go.

Dodger Come with me! I know a gentleman who'll give you lodgings for free!

The Dodger led Oliver through narrow, winding streets to a dirty old house. There, Oliver met Fagin — an old man with a wrinkled face and greedy eyes, surrounded by a group of boys.

👴🧒👦Fagin's Den Fagin So, my dear, you want to stay with us? Very well! You'll be comfortable here, I promise.

Fagin gave Oliver food and a place to sleep. But Oliver didn't yet know that Fagin and his boys were pickpockets — they stole wallets and handkerchiefs from rich people in the streets.

Chapter 4 · A Terrible Mistake

🎩👦🏃The Pickpocket One day, the Dodger took Oliver for a walk. The Dodger spotted a wealthy old gentleman reading a book at a bookstall. Quick as a flash, the Dodger picked the gentleman's pocket and ran. But the gentleman turned around just as Oliver was standing there, confused.

Gentleman (Mr. Brownlow) Stop! Thief! My handkerchief is gone!

People grab Oliver and drag him through the streets. A crowd gathers.

Poor Oliver was dragged before a magistrate. But a kind bookstall owner had seen everything and testified that Oliver was innocent — it was another boy who had stolen the handkerchief. The gentleman, Mr. Brownlow, felt terrible for the frightened boy.

Mr. Brownlow The poor child looks half-starved! Come home with me, boy. I'll take care of you.

For the first time in his life, Oliver experienced kindness. Mr. Brownlow gave him clean clothes, good food, and a warm bed. Oliver was happy and hoped he would never have to return to Fagin's den.

Chapter 5 · Captured Again

😈👦🔒The Capture But Fagin was angry. Oliver knew too much about their operations. Fagin sent a terrifying man named Bill Sikes and a woman named Nancy to find Oliver and bring him back.

Bill Sikes Come along, boy! Your friends are waiting for you!

Sikes grabs Oliver roughly. Nancy looks away, her heart heavy with guilt.

Nancy was a kind woman trapped in a terrible life. She secretly helped Oliver and tried to protect him from Sikes's cruelty. But Sikes was a violent man who trusted no one.

Nancy (whispering to Oliver) I'll help you, child. I promise. But you must be patient.

Sikes forced Oliver to help him break into a house one dark night. But the plan went wrong — the homeowners woke up and raised the alarm. Sikes escaped, but Oliver was shot and left behind, wounded and bleeding.

Chapter 6 · A Kind Rescue

🏠❤️👦The Rescue The people in the house — the kind Mrs. Maylie and her beautiful niece Rose — found Oliver bleeding in the garden. Instead of calling the police, they carried him inside and nursed him back to health.

Mrs. Maylie This poor child is innocent. Look at his face — there is no evil in him.

Rose We'll take care of you, Oliver. You're safe now.

Oliver stayed with the Maylies in their beautiful country home. He learned to read and write, played in the garden, and felt truly loved for the first time. But Fagin and Sikes were still searching for him.

Meanwhile, Nancy, tormented by guilt, secretly met with Rose and Mr. Brownlow. She told them everything about Fagin's plans to recapture Oliver. But Sikes discovered her betrayal.

Sikes, furious and wild, confronts Nancy in a dark room.

Sikes You've betrayed me, Nancy! You told them everything!

Nancy I did what was right! Oliver is just a child — he deserves a better life!

In a terrible rage, Sikes attacked Nancy. Her brave sacrifice would not be in vain — the police were now hunting for Fagin and his gang.
